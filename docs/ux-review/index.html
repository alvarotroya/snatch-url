round 3

The one you just asked for

You said: keep the popup, make it wider, like a secondary address bar below the real one, smarter and with more functions. A Chrome action popup is capped at 800 × 600 and is anchored under the toolbar icon — so a wide, shallow one lands exactly where you described. E is 800 wide and about 190 tall with the drawer shut.

E

Second Address Bar

your newest idea

A Chrome popup is capped at 800 × 600 and hangs directly under the toolbar icon — which is to say, directly under the real address bar. Make it 800 wide and 190 tall and it stops being a panel and becomes a second address bar.

E · Second Address Bar open in its own tab ↗
Try this
  1. The whole URL fits in three lines. Click price_desc right in the bar and type over it.
  2. Click the QUERY 7 params ▾ cell — a shallow drawer opens under the bar, not a panel over it.
  3. Switch the demo URL to monster and press ⚡ Clean — three utm_* parameters gone, staged, revertible.
  4. Press decoded in the title bar: the bar shows what the URL says; edits still write proper encoding.
  5. Press one line for the purest address-bar shape — it scrolls sideways like the real one.
  6. Open the ref row with ⌄ to see the nested URL peeled.

What it makes easy

  • It is the shape you asked for. Closed it is ~190px tall and reads as a second address bar; the mock browser above it is there so you can see that.
  • The whole URL is visible at 800px — three lines for the demo URL, where the 440px popup needed six and still cut it off.
  • Sections are cells with carets, exactly your "dropdowns for each section": host, path, query, fragment, each showing a count, each one click from its rows.
  • Two levels of action. Section-level in the drawer header (copy section raw, copy decoded, clear); part-level per row (peel, copy, delete) and in the bar itself (click to type over).
  • "More functions" is where the width pays. ⚡ Clean strips tracking params, decoded flips the whole bar to plain text, + param adds one, and the whole URL copies from a single button.
  • Staged, not live. Same Apply / New tab / Revert as the others; nothing navigates until you say so.

What it makes hard

  • 800px is wide. On a 1280px laptop the popup covers most of the window. It is a popup, so it also dies the moment you click the page behind it — the thing it most resembles is the one thing it cannot be.
  • Still no character selection. The bar is a row of spans, not a text field, so you cannot drag a selection from the middle of one part into the next. That is A · Live Line's trick, and it is not in here.
  • Tiny targets. tag=a gives you a one-character click target. The drawer rescues it, but the bar alone is fiddly for short parts.
  • The drawer repeats the bar. Same parts, twice, in two places — the same complaint C · Split earns, in a milder form.
  • The mock browser is a demo device. A real popup cannot dim the page or draw the address bar above itself; it will look slightly less integrated than this does.
round 2

Horizontal, with dropdowns and quick actions

Four readings of “displayed horizontally with dropdowns for each section, quick actions for each section”, each answering a different question: does the action belong in a menu (D), under your pointer (F), in one fixed place (G), or behind what you type (H)?

D

Rail

the literal reading

The URL as one horizontal rail of sections, each a cell with a caret; clicking one drops a panel of its parts with quick actions.

D · Rail open in its own tab ↗
Try this
  1. Click ? 7 params ▾ — every parameter, numbered, with copy / edit / delete on each row.
  2. Press Copy in the panel header for the whole query as URL text; All selects every row.
  3. Edit a row: the text comes up already selected, so typing replaces it.
  4. Flip the page-level size switch to 800 wide — the rail stops scrolling and all four sections sit on one line.

What it makes easy

  • Immediately legible. Four cells, four counts; you know what the URL contains before you touch anything.
  • The panel is roomy — numbered rows, full values, badges, and per-row actions that do not hide behind a hover.
  • Section-level actions (Copy / All / + Add / Clear) sit where the section is, which is what you asked for.
  • A URL preview strip at the bottom keeps the real string in sight and copyable.

What it makes hard

  • At 440px the rail scrolls sideways and you cannot see all four sections at once — the design wants the wider popup.
  • One section at a time. Comparing a path segment against a query value means two clicks and a lost place.
  • The URL and its parts are never in the same view except as that dim preview strip.
  • No character selection, and no way to act on parts from two different sections together.
F

Hover Cards

no menus at all

The URL stays the whole interface. Point at any part and its actions come to you in a small card.

F · Hover Cards open in its own tab ↗
Try this
  1. Hover price_desc — a card appears under it with the decoded value and Copy / Edit / Delete.
  2. Click a part instead to pin the card, so you can reach the buttons without careful hovering.
  3. Hover the QUERY pill at the top to light up that whole run of the URL.
  4. Hover the ref value and use inside to peel the nested URL.

What it makes easy

  • Nothing is in the way. No permanent toolbar, no drawer; the URL gets the whole popup.
  • The action is where your eye already is — no travel between the part and its buttons.
  • Pinning fixes the usual hover complaint: click and the card stays put.
  • Section pills give whole-portion selection without spending vertical space.

What it makes hard

  • Fiddly at 440px. Short parts are small targets and the card can land over its neighbours. It is markedly better at 800.
  • Nothing is discoverable. A first-time user sees a coloured URL and no affordances at all until they happen to hover.
  • Hover is not a real input on touch, and it is awkward for keyboard and screen-reader users — the weakest of the five on accessibility.
  • One part at a time. No multi-select, so no "copy these three parameters".
G

Action Bar

muscle memory

One action bar, always in the same place. Select a section or a part and the bar's buttons swap — nothing ever opens or closes.

G · Action Bar open in its own tab ↗
Try this
  1. Click price_desc in the URL — the bar below lights up with Copy / Decoded / Edit / Dup / Delete / Decode.
  2. Now press C. Every button carries its key, and the keys work whenever something is selected.
  3. Shift-click a neighbouring part to extend the selection, then Copy — they join the way the URL joins them.
  4. Click the QUERY pill for section-level actions in the same bar.

What it makes easy

  • The actions never move. After five minutes you stop reading the buttons and just hit the keys — the strongest of the five for a tool you open fifty times a day.
  • Nothing opens or closes, so there is no state to lose and no menu to dismiss.
  • Multi-select with shift-click, and the bar tells you how many parts you have.
  • Keyboard shortcuts are printed on the buttons, so the fast path teaches itself.

What it makes hard

  • It spends permanent vertical space on a bar that is empty until you select something.
  • Everything is two steps — select, then act. D and F both get there in one.
  • Six buttons at 440px is cramped; labels shrink to near-glyphs.
  • No character selection, and the bar has no room for the decode stack, so Decode pushes a strip in underneath.
H

Command Bar

for the keyboard

The URL on top, one input underneath. Type to find a part, press a key to act on it. The input doubles as the editor.

H · Command Bar open in its own tab ↗
Try this
  1. Type sort — the matching parameter lights up in the URL above and appears as the only result.
  2. Press Enter: the same input becomes "edit param value" with the value armed. Type and commit.
  3. Type utm_source=newsletter and press Enter to add a parameter — or set one that already exists.
  4. Try the prefixes: > commands, ? query, / path, # fragment, @ host.

What it makes easy

  • By far the fastest once you know it. Find and edit are the same gesture in the same field.
  • It scales to the monster URL better than anything else here — 13 parameters is a non-event when you type two letters to reach one.
  • Add and set are one syntax. name=value adds it, or replaces the value if the name exists.
  • The URL stays highlighted above, so you always see where the thing you are editing lives.

What it makes hard

  • The language is invisible. Five prefixes to learn, and nothing on screen forces you to learn them.
  • A mouse user gets almost nothing — the section pills, and that is it.
  • The results list pushes the URL up the moment you type, so the thing you are editing moves.
  • It is the least "touching it with your hands" of the eight. It is fast, but it is a command line wearing a URL.
round 1

The first three, still standing

These answered the original brief. A is still the only one where you can select arbitrary characters, and B is still the best with an awful URL — both are worth stealing from whatever wins.

A

Live Line

round 1 · the literal reading

The URL is the input: one editable line, coloured in place, with a real caret in it.

A · Live Line open in its own tab ↗
Try this
  1. Drag across %3A%2F%2F — an arbitrary run of characters, exactly as in any text field.
  2. Double-click a part to snap the selection to it; Alt+↑ grows part → parameter → query → whole URL.
  3. Select a value and type: the space becomes %20 as it lands.

What it makes easy

  • The only one that does character selection, because it is real text.
  • What you see is what gets navigated to — no translation in your head.
  • Typing punctuation restructures the URL: & makes a parameter, / splits a segment.

What it makes hard

  • Raw is unreadable — the panel decodes whatever you stand on, but the line itself stays ugly.
  • Aiming on a long URL is fiddly; the keyboard hops are the real answer.
  • You can break it, and no drag-to-reorder.
B

Bricks

round 1 · the departure

The URL is not text, it is a handful of objects: pick them up, reorder them, pull one apart, walk inside it.

B · Bricks open in its own tab ↗
Try this
  1. Click tags, then ⇧-click debug → four parameters selected; Copy gives real URL text.
  2. Drag catalog before eu. Dashed yellow means "moved, unapplied".
  3. Click ref → Inside: the nested URL opens as its own bricks, then Put it back.

What it makes easy

  • Best density of the eight — the 13-parameter monster URL fits with no scrolling at all.
  • Drag to reorder, multi-select copy, split a delimited value, walk inside a nested URL.
  • Deletions stay on screen as ghosts you can click to restore.

What it makes hard

  • No character selection across bricks — the one thing in your original list it cannot do.
  • No URL text to trust, so it needs the dim preview strip at the bottom.
  • New vocabulary to learn, and punctuation is not yours to type.
C

Split

round 1 · the two-way

A real text field of the raw URL over a list of parts, wired both ways: select characters and the part lights up, click a part and its characters get selected.

C · Split open in its own tab ↗
Try this
  1. Drag across characters in the top line — the strip names what you grabbed and the matching row lights teal.
  2. Hover a row to glow its characters above; click it to really select them.
  3. Paste a completely different URL into the top line — it takes itself apart as you type.

What it makes easy

  • Both kinds of selection with no trade: characters in the field, parts in the list.
  • It teaches the structure — the only design that shows you the mapping.
  • Paste any URL to take it apart.

What it makes hard

  • Two views of one URL cost double the height — the worst fit for a short bar.
  • Two right answers for every edit: up there or down here?
  • The bottom half is still a form.

Two decisions, not eight

Every prototype here is a choice of shell (how the URL is laid out and how much room it gets) crossed with an action mechanism (how you reach what you can do to a part). They mix freely — that is what makes this a two-question decision rather than a beauty contest.

What I would build

E's shell. You were right that the width changes the thing: at 800 the whole URL is simply there, and the popup stops looking like a panel and starts looking like part of the browser. Everything else gets easier once the URL fits.

Plus G's action bar instead of E's drawer. The drawer is the weakest part of E — it repeats the bar and doubles the height. A fixed action row in the same place, with the keys printed on it, keeps the popup short and gets rid of the "same thing twice" problem. Plus H's input as an optional line for the power path: type two letters to reach a parameter instead of aiming at a one-character target.

The one thing none of that gives you is dragging a selection through the URL. If that matters, the bar has to be a real text field — that is A, and it costs the coloured section cells. Question 3 below asks you exactly that.

Your call

Four questions

Answer what you have an opinion on and press Record on each. Your answers collect in the box at the bottom, ready to copy to whoever builds this next. Skipping one is a fine answer — the default gets taken and said out loud.

1 · Which shell?

How the URL is laid out, and how much room the popup takes.

2 · How do you reach the actions?

This is independent of question 1 — any of these can live in any shell.

3 · Do you still want to drag-select characters?

It is the one thing the wide bar cannot do. Coloured section cells and a real text caret are mutually exclusive — a text field cannot hold buttons, and spans cannot hold a caret.
